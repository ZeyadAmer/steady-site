Summary

  • Steady keeps the information you put into it so your household can use it together. That is the only reason we have it.
  • There are no ads in Steady. We do not sell your personal data. Steady launches with no third-party analytics or tracking.
  • Private items, like cycle tracking and a person’s medical record, are seen only by their owner and the people the owner chooses. Parents can see and edit their children’s medical records.
  • Location is collected only when the Map is used and you allow it. It can include your location in the background.
  • Your data is stored with Supabase in the European Union. A few service providers help us run the app, and we list them below.
  • You can see, correct, export and delete your data. You can delete your account by writing to us. [TO CONFIRM: in-app account deletion, to be built before launch]

2. What we collect

We collect only what the app needs to work.

Account details
Your email address if you sign in with a one-time code. If you use Sign in with Apple, the app asks Apple for your name and email address, and Apple gives us an identifier for your account. Apple lets you hide your real email address, and then gives us a private relay address that forwards to it. A child who joins with a code from a parent does not need an email address.
Household details
The names of the people in your household and their roles, such as parent or child. A parent can add a child who has no phone as a profile.
What you enter
Grocery and pantry items, recipes, reminders, countdowns, birthdays, notes, workout logs, medical test results and cycle tracking entries. It also includes household information you choose to store, such as account numbers or Wi‑Fi details.
Location
Your precise location, including in the background, for the family map. We collect it only when the Map is used and you have given location permission. See section 7.
Apple Health data
Only if you choose to connect Apple Health and give permission. We read only the types of data you allow. See section 8.
Kitchen
The household’s lists and the things on them, places and sections, counts, optional use-by dates, recipes, shop trips, and children’s requests with their parents’ answers.
Dates and reminders
The household’s countdowns and birthdays, with a birth year only if someone enters one, and its reminders: the title, an optional note, the day and time, how it repeats, who it is for, whether it is hidden on the lock screen, and the time zone of the phone it was set on. For each person: the reminders they marked done and when, their own choices for birthday and countdown alerts, and, for each reminder they are on, whether their phone has set it to ring and whether it can ring.
Device token
A push token that Apple gives your phone, with the phone’s time zone and language, the notification choices made on it, and each person’s own choices for birthday and countdown alerts, so we can send the notifications you asked for at the right local time and in your language.
Kitchen widget
To show the Kitchen widget on the Home Screen and lock screen, the app keeps a small copy of the list: how many things are on it and the names and quantities of the first 4, or, on a child’s phone, how many of their own requests wait and their 3 newest. This copy is kept on your phone only, in a space shared only with the widget, and is never sent anywhere. It is emptied when you sign out. Steady’s server knows only whether a phone shows the widget, so that it can send that phone a notification with no text when the list changes, which lets the widget update.
Countdown widget
To show the countdown widget, the app keeps a small copy of the household’s countdowns (the title, date, how it repeats, symbol and colour) and of its birthdays (the name, the day and any birth year; on a child’s phone, no birth year but the child’s own). It holds no reminder, note or alert choice. Like the Kitchen widget’s copy, it is kept on your phone only, in a space shared only with the widget, and is never sent anywhere. It is emptied when you sign out.
Subscription status
Which plan your household has and whether it is active. Apple handles payment. We never see your card details.
Messages to us
If you email us, we receive your email address and what you write.

Voice notes (Kitchen, parents only). When a parent records a voice note, the recording, together with the names of the household’s Kitchen places, sections and things, is sent through Steady’s server to an AI service provider (named in the list of processors below), which turns it into text. That text, with the household’s Kitchen place and item names, is then sent to the same provider to be sorted into items for the parent to check. Steady does not store the recording or the text. The provider processes them only to provide this service. [TO CONFIRM: Groq’s free-tier data use and retention] Voice notes are never used for advertising or tracking, and can be turned off in Kitchen settings.

Steady keeps only a short record of each voice note, without any of the words, for 30 days: when it was made, by which member of the household, how long it was, how it ended and how many lines it gave. It is used for the limits on voice notes: per day, per minute and attempts per day. Daily totals across all households, with no household or person in them, are kept for a year to see how well voice notes work.

During testing only. While Steady is being tested before its public release, the voice notes of a small number of test accounts belonging to the developer’s own household, who have agreed and see a note on screen saying so, are kept: the recording, the text and the sorted result. They are kept in Steady’s private database for at most 90 days, only to improve how well voice notes understand, and no one can read them in the app. No other account’s voice notes are kept. This ends before the public release.

Recipe ideas (Kitchen, parents only). When a parent asks for recipe ideas, Steady sends, through its server, the names and counts of things at home, anything typed in the ideas box and the language for the answer to an AI service provider (named in the list of processors below), to get suggestions. It sends at most 150 things, each name cut to 60 characters. It does not send names of people, account details, places, sections, dates, requests, recipes, or anything from outside Kitchen. If someone typed a person’s name into a thing’s name or the ideas box, that name is sent with it. Steady keeps no copy of what is sent or of the answer (unless a parent saves an idea as a recipe), only a short record of each request, with no words in it, for 2 days, and how many ideas a household has used each day, for 30 days. Each person sees an explanation on their phone before the first use, and a parent can turn recipe ideas off for the household in Kitchen settings; when off, nothing is sent.

We do not collect advertising identifiers, and we do not build profiles of you for marketing.

3. Why we use it

  • To run the app. To sign you in, keep your household’s lists, records and reminders, and show them to the right people.
  • To show your household its shared kitchen: what is at home, the shopping list, recipes and children’s requests.
  • To show the family map, when your household uses it.
  • To send notifications you have asked for, such as reminders, and your household’s own Kitchen notes: someone is at the shop, a request to pick things up, things added while you shop, a child’s request and its answer, and, if you turn it on, a daily count of things to use soon. These notes never contain item names. No marketing notifications.
  • To manage your subscription, including the free trial and what happens when a subscription ends.
  • To help you when you contact us.
  • To keep the service safe and to meet legal obligations.

We do not use your data for advertising.

4. Who can see it in your household

Steady is made for sharing within a household. What each person sees depends on their role.

  • Shared items, like the grocery list or reminders, are shared within the household according to roles.
  • Private items, like cycle tracking or a person’s medical record, are visible only to their owner and the people the owner chooses. There is one exception: parents can see and edit their children’s medical records.
  • Children see a limited version of the app. Parents decide which parts of the household they can see.
  • Dates and reminders. Everyone in the household sees its countdowns and birthdays. A child’s phone also receives the birth years entered, but shows no one’s age except the child’s own. Parents see every reminder, who has marked it done and whether each person’s phone can ring it. A child’s phone receives only the reminders for everyone or that name the child, and only the child’s own done marks. When a child is taken off a reminder, their phone removes it and keeps none of its content. A parent’s birth year can be entered only by that parent, and a child’s by a parent. Each person’s alert choices are seen by that person only.
  • Location. Parents can see their children’s location. A child cannot turn off their own location sharing in the app.

These rules are enforced in the database, not only hidden on the screen.

5. Who we share it with

We do not sell your personal data, and we do not share it with advertisers. We use a small number of service providers to run Steady. They handle data on our behalf, only to provide their service.

Supabase
Database, sign-in and file storage. Your data is hosted in the European Union, in the Paris region.
Apple
Sign in with Apple, payments for subscriptions, and push notifications. Apple Push Notification service delivers Steady’s notifications: it receives the phone’s push token, the notification’s text, and the kind of note with internal ids that tell the app what it is about, never a name. A Kitchen note is a short text that can name the household member it is about, but never the name of a thing. A note that a reminder was set for you shows its title, its day or how it repeats, and its time, with the name of the person who set it (“New reminder from Mona”); a note that it changed shows the same without a name (“Reminder changed”). If the reminder is hidden on the lock screen, the note says only “New reminder from Mona.” or “A reminder changed.”. Apple’s own privacy policy applies to what Apple handles.
RevenueCat
Subscription management. RevenueCat receives an app user identifier and information about your purchases.
Email delivery
A provider that sends your one-time sign-in codes. It receives your email address and the code. [TO CONFIRM: email delivery provider’s name and location]
Groq
The AI service provider for voice notes and recipe ideas in Kitchen (see section 2). For voice notes, it receives the recording with the names of the household’s Kitchen places, sections and things, then the text with the Kitchen place and item names, and sends back the sorted list. For recipe ideas, it receives the names and counts of things at home and anything typed in the ideas box, and sends back the ideas. It receives no account details. [TO CONFIRM: Groq’s free-tier data use and retention]

Dates and reminders. Nothing of a reminder leaves the household’s own phones and Steady’s database, except the note to Apple described above when a reminder is set for someone or changed. Reminders and the alerts before birthdays and countdowns are set on each phone as its own notifications, from what that phone already holds: they ring without a connection, and nothing is sent anywhere for them. They show on the lock screen: a reminder with its title, and under it its note and who set it, or whichever of the two it has, or, with neither, when it repeats, unless it is hidden on the lock screen (then only “You have a reminder.”); a birthday or countdown alert with the person’s or the countdown’s name (and, on a parent’s phone, the age a person turns). Suggestions, Hijri dates and the first day of the week are worked out on the phone, and nothing is sent for them.

The app checks Steady’s own storage, at Supabase, for updates to its content, and downloads them from there. There is no third-party update service, and the check sends no account or household details.

We may also disclose information if the law requires it, or to protect someone’s safety. [TO CONFIRM: wording of legal-disclosure and business-transfer clauses]

6. Children

Steady is used by families, and children may use it under the supervision of a parent. A parent creates or approves a child’s membership in the household and consents on the child’s behalf.

  • A child can join with a code from a parent, without an email address.
  • A parent can add a child who has no phone as a profile. The child does not sign in.
  • Children see a limited app. Parents can see their children’s location and medical records.
  • A child can send short requests to their own parents and see the answers. A child’s phone never receives the household’s list, places or recipes. Recipe ideas and voice notes are not available to children.
  • Steady shows no ads to anyone, including children.

A parent can ask to see, correct or delete a child’s data at any time, in the app or by writing to us. [TO CONFIRM: how parental consent is collected and verified, and the age thresholds that apply in each market]

7. Location

The family map uses your precise location, including in the background, so your household can see where you are. We collect location only when the Map is used and you have given location permission on your iPhone.

You can stop sharing at any time by turning off location access for Steady in your iPhone settings. In the app, a child cannot turn off their own location sharing; that choice belongs to the child’s parents.

We use location only to show the family map. We do not sell it or use it for advertising.

8. Health and cycle data

Medical test results, cycle tracking entries and any data read from Apple Health are sensitive. We treat them that way.

  • Health and cycle data, including Apple Health data, are never used for advertising.
  • They are never shared with third parties, apart from the service providers we need to store them.
  • Cycle tracking is private to the person tracking, unless they choose to share it.
  • Apple Health data is read only with your permission, and you can withdraw it in your iPhone settings.

9. Where data is stored

A copy of your household’s basic details is kept on your phone, so the app opens without a connection: the household’s name and plan, its members’ names and roles, and which sections are switched on. It is removed from the phone when you sign out in the app. If you are signed out in another way (for example, when Steady’s server ends your sign-in), the copy is no longer shown once the phone learns of it, and stays on the phone at most until the next sign-in there: it is removed if someone else signs in, and replaced with a fresh copy if you do.

The app also keeps on your phone a copy of the parts of the household you can see that work without a connection, such as Kitchen, with any changes not yet sent, so that they work offline. This copy is removed from the phone when you sign out in the app. If you are signed out in another way, it is no longer shown, nothing in it is sent, and it stays on the phone until the next sign-in there: if you sign in again to the same household with the same role, your changes not yet sent are sent then; if anyone else signs in, or you in another household or role, it is removed before anything in it is shown.

Your data is stored by Supabase in the European Union (Paris region). Some service providers, such as Apple, RevenueCat and the AI service provider for voice notes and recipe ideas, may process data in other countries, including the United States. 10. How long we keep it

We keep your data while your account exists, so the app can work.

  • If a subscription ends, the household becomes read-only and its data is kept. [TO CONFIRM: how long data is kept after a subscription ends]
  • If you delete your account, your private data is deleted. Shared household items you created stay with the household. [TO CONFIRM: time to complete deletion, including backups]
  • Kitchen: things, places, sections and recipes that are deleted are kept for 180 days, so that a phone that was offline does not bring them back, and are then removed by a daily clean-up.
  • Dates and reminders: countdowns, birthdays, reminders and done marks that are deleted (or undone) are kept for 180 days, so that a phone that was offline does not bring them back, and are then removed by a daily clean-up. A deleted reminder’s done marks and ring records are removed with it.
  • Notifications: notification records are deleted 7 days after they are created, or, for one that was still waiting to be sent when it expired, 7 days after it expired. The record that the day’s use-soon note was sent is deleted after 3 days.
  • Shop trips: a shop trip is removed from everyone’s phones a day after it ends, with the shop’s name cleared. After 7 days nothing left of it says who went, for whom or when, and what remains is deleted with the other removed Kitchen items.
  • Children’s requests: a request that was declined, withdrawn or bought is removed 90 days after it was answered (for a thing that was bought, counted from the answer, not from the purchase), with its note and any reason given cleared, and what remains is deleted with the other removed Kitchen items. A request still waiting, or added to the list and not yet bought, is kept.
  • Recipe ideas: the record of each request, which holds no words, is deleted after 2 days, and the daily counts after 30 days.
  • Voice notes: the short record of each note is deleted after 30 days, and the daily totals, which name no household or person, after about a year. During testing only, the kept recordings of the test accounts are deleted after 90 days, by a daily clean-up that can take up to a day more.
  • Emails to support are kept for [TO CONFIRM: retention period for support emails].

No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot promise that data will never be exposed. If something goes wrong, we will tell affected people as the law requires.
